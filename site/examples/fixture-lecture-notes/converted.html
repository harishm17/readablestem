Chapter 2: Motion in One Dimension

These notes describe motion along a straight line. We track where an object is, how far it has moved, and how quickly its position changes. Every quantity comes with a unit, and checking units is the fastest way to catch a mistake in a calculation.

2.1 Position and Displacement

Position is measured from a chosen origin. When a cart moves from an initial position x0 to a final position xf, its displacement is the change in position:

Δx=xf−x0

Displacement has a direction. A cart that ends up behind its starting point has a negative displacement, even though the distance it travelled is positive.

Units

Positions and displacements are measured in metres. A displacement of 3 m to the left is written as −3 m when the positive direction points right.

2.2 Speed and Velocity

Average velocity is displacement divided by the time taken. For a trip that lasts Δt, the average velocity is v=Δx/Δt, and its unit is metres per second.

v=ΔxΔt

If we know the kinetic energy of the cart and its mass, its speed follows from

v=2Km

2.3 Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate at which velocity changes. A cart that speeds up from rest to 6 m/s in two seconds has an average acceleration of 3 m/s2.

a=ΔvΔt

When the acceleration is constant, the velocity grows by the same amount every second and a graph of velocity against time is a straight line.

Free fall

Near the surface of the Earth, a dropped ball has a nearly constant downward acceleration g as long as air resistance is small. Timing a fall over a known height is a simple way to estimate it in the lab.
