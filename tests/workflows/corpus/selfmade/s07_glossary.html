Glossary

A

Abiotic factor. A non-living part of an ecosystem, such as sunlight, temperature or soil minerals.

B

Biodiversity. The variety of living things in a habitat, a region or on Earth.

Biome. A large region with a characteristic climate and community of plants and animals.

C

Carbon cycle. The movement of carbon among the air, water, soil and living things.

Carrying capacity. The largest population that an environment can support over a long time.

Community. All of the populations of different species that live in the same area.

D

Decomposer. An organism, such as a fungus or bacterium, that breaks down dead matter.

E

Ecosystem. A community of living things together with the non-living environment they interact with.

Eutrophication. Excess growth of algae caused by too many nutrients in water, often leading to low oxygen.

F

Food web. A network of overlapping food chains showing who eats whom in an ecosystem.

H

Habitat. The place where an organism normally lives, with the food, water and shelter it needs.

K

Keystone species. A species that has a much larger effect on its community than its numbers would suggest.

L

Limiting factor. A resource or condition that restricts the growth of a population.

N

Nitrogen fixation. The conversion of nitrogen gas into forms that plants can use, done by certain bacteria.

Niche. The role of a species in its community, including what it eats and where it lives.

P

Population. A group of individuals of the same species living in the same area.

Producer. An organism that makes its own food, usually by photosynthesis.

S

Succession. The gradual change in the species of a community over time after a disturbance.

T

Trophic level. A feeding position in a food chain, such as producer or primary consumer.

W

Watershed. The land area that drains into a particular river, lake or ocean.
