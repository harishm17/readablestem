What it does not do yet

  • Scanned pages are not read. There is no text recognition (OCR): a PDF made only of scanned pages is refused, and scanned pages in a PDF with text are kept as images, marked “Not converted”. Run OCR first, for example in Adobe Acrobat.
  • Handwriting and chemistry notation are not converted.
  • Two-column pages and slides convert less well than single-column notes: check the reading order.
  • Size. Up to {{ max_pages }} pages and {{ max_upload_mb }} MB per upload. Split longer documents.
  • Your documents are deleted {{ retention_hours }} hours after upload ({{ opt_in_days }} days if you choose to keep them longer). Download the file before then.
  • The checks look at structure, not meaning. Every file is checked for structure (language, title, headings, lists, image text alternatives). The full axe-core accessibility test runs on our own test documents, not on yours, and nothing here replaces testing with a screen reader.

How accurate it is

Measured on a small sample only: three sections of one high-school physics textbook (OpenStax Physics, chapter 2), 43 PDF pages, on 8 October 2026.

  • Equations: 23 of 30 came out exactly right (95% interval: 59% to 88%).
  • Headings: all 121 were found; 120 of them at the right level.
  • Decisions it asked for: about 6 in 10 pointed at a real problem.

Real course PDFs, especially ones typeset with LaTeX or exported from Word, may do worse. The evaluation report says how this was measured and what the numbers do not show.
