A 2024 Justice Department rule under the ADA asks public universities to make their online course materials accessible to students with disabilities. In STEM the hard part is the maths. a11y-stem assists with it: upload a PDF of your own notes, slides or problem sets, and get back one web page where:

  • equations are real maths (MathML) that a screen reader reads aloud, for example “v equals d over t”, and lets students explore,
  • headings are real headings, in a repaired outline,
  • figures have short alt text, or a clear flag where a description is still needed.

How long it takes

Converting takes a few minutes. Then you decide only what could change what a student reads; everything else is accepted for you and listed, so you can look it over. In our tests a 16-page textbook section needed 3 decisions, and a one-page handout 1 or 2 (it varies from run to run).

How accurate it is

It makes mistakes, so look over the equations your students rely on. In our small test, 23 of 30 equations from one physics textbook came out exactly right, and about 6 in 10 of the decisions it asked for pointed at a real problem. Its checks against the PDF's own text catch some wrong equations, not all. How we measured this, and what the numbers do not show.

What you do with the file

Post it in your course in place of the PDF. Students open it in any browser. How to post it in your course.

Who can use it

The beta is open to a small group of instructors, by invitation. Upload only materials you wrote or have the right to adapt, and never upload student records or anything with students' personal information in it.

What happens to your documents

Your PDF and everything made from it are deleted after {{ retention_hours }} hours, or after {{ opt_in_days }} days if you choose to let us keep them to improve the tool. You can delete them yourself at any time. Uploads are never used to train AI models. a11y-stem assists you; it does not replace your judgement or a full accessibility review of your course.
