Turn STEM course PDFs into accessible web pages, equations included

{# No deadline date is stated here: the rule is 28 CFR part 35, subpart H, and a date should only be added once it is checked against it and the university's own page (UX review BW9). #}

Upload your lecture notes, slides or problem sets. ReadableSTEM rebuilds each equation as real maths (MathML) that a screen reader can speak, restores the headings and describes the figures.

When it is unsure about something, it asks you on a review screen, where you accept, edit or reject the change. Then you download a web page to post in your course.

How each works, and what it does not do yet.

Upload, review, post

  1. Upload

    Upload up to {{ max_pages }} pages at a time. Converting takes a few minutes, and you can open a sample document first to see the review screen.

  2. Review

    You decide only what could change what a student reads. Everything else is accepted and listed for you to look over. Once your document is converted, its page tells you exactly how many decisions it needs.

  3. Post

    Post the web page in your course in place of the PDF. Students open it in any browser and do not sign in. How to post it in your course.

“Exactly right” means the whole equation matches the original symbol for symbol. The first score also counts an equation as right when a screen reader reads it the same way as the original. The rest is why you review the equations the tool flags.

On equations, this test found no difference it could detect between ReadableSTEM and a general chatbot. Given the same PDFs, a chatbot (Claude Opus) scored 0.836 against 0.840 for ReadableSTEM (26 documents, 1,715 equations). ReadableSTEM left fewer wrong equations without a question to you (5.0 against 9.6 per 100), but not as few as the goal we set, and it asked about 46% of its wrong equations against a goal of 60%. In 3 chemistry sections, 0.33 of the equations were right as chemistry, against a goal of 0.80. Earlier tests (October 2026, version 0.4.0) are kept as history in the full results.

Its checks compare each equation with the PDF's own text and flag the ones that disagree. They catch some wrong equations, not all. All the results, how they were measured and what they do not show.

What happens to your documents

Your file and everything made from it are deleted {{ retention_hours }} hours after you last work on them: each decision or download starts the clock again. You can choose to keep a document for 7 days, at upload, from its page, or for every upload in your account settings, seen only by you and the people you name. You can also let us look at the results for {{ opt_in_days }} days, to find and fix mistakes in the tool. You can delete a document at any time. We do not use your uploads to train models. Read the privacy notice.

Why STEM courses need this now

The Department of Justice's ADA Title II web rule asks public universities to make their online course materials accessible to students with disabilities. In STEM the hard part is the maths: a PDF equation is usually a picture or a jumble of characters to a screen reader. What the rule asks of STEM course materials.

For accessibility and disability services offices

ReadableSTEM assists the instructor. It does not replace their judgement or your office's review, and it does not say that a document meets WCAG, the ADA or Section 508. The page for accessibility offices covers where a document goes, who receives it, how long it is kept, and a pilot checklist you can print.
