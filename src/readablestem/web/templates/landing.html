Turn STEM course PDFs into accessible web pages, equations included

{# No deadline date is stated here: the rule is 28 CFR part 35, subpart H, and a date should only be added once it is checked against it and the university's own page (UX review BW9). #}

Upload your lecture notes, slides or problem sets. ReadableSTEM rebuilds each equation as MathML that a screen reader can speak, restores the headings and describes the figures.

When it is unsure about something, it asks you on a review screen, where you accept, edit or reject the change. Then you download a web page to post in your course.

What it handles

  • PDFs typeset with LaTeX or exported from Word{% if office_on %}, and Word (.docx) and PowerPoint (.pptx) files, which it turns into a PDF first{% endif %}.
  • Equations in running text and on their own line. Each becomes MathML, so a screen reader says “v equals d over t” and a student can step through it.
  • Chemistry: formulas, ions and reactions are written as chemistry, and the review screen reads them the way a chemistry teacher says them: “2 H 2 plus O 2 reacts to form 2 H 2 O”. This is its weakest area, so check each one.
  • Beamer slide decks, read frame by frame. Overlay steps that only add a line are merged into one slide, and each frame title becomes a heading.
    • {% if tables_on %}
  • Tables, rebuilt as real tables with header cells. A table it cannot read stays a picture of the original, and it asks you about it.
    • {% endif %}
  • Figures, with short alt text{% if long_on %}, and a longer description for a chart or a diagram that needs one{% endif %}. For an exam or quiz, descriptions leave out the answers.
    • {% if image_pages_on %}
  • Scanned and handwritten pages, when you tick a box at upload. Each page is read from its picture, and you check every equation before you share the file.
    • {% endif %} {% if notation_on %}
  • Your course's notation. Tell it once what your symbols mean, and every document you upload for that course uses it.
    • {% endif %} {# When LaTeX source upload is turned on (latex_source_on), list it here. #}

Some things it does not do yet, such as maths in headings or a table without ruled lines. See the known limits.

Upload, review, post

  1. Upload

    Upload up to {{ max_pages }} pages at a time. Converting takes a few minutes, and you can open a sample document first to see the review screen.

  2. Review

    You decide only what could change what a student reads. On textbook sections that was about 1.8 decisions per page; real course PDFs may need more. Everything else is accepted and listed for you to look over.

  3. Post

    Post the web page in your course in place of the PDF. Students open it in any browser and do not sign in. How to post it in your course.

What you download

  • A web page: one .html file with the text, equations and pictures inside it. This is the version to post.
  • The same document as {% if word_export %}Word (.docx) or {% endif %}EPUB, with the equations kept as equations. “Download all” puts every finished document in one file.
  • A zip for a Canvas page, and a Common Cartridge to import into Canvas, Blackboard, Moodle or D2L. Neither has been tried in a live course yet.
  • A record of the review, to print or open as a CSV: what the tool proposed and what you decided.

You can share a document with a colleague or a TA by email address. They can view it, or help you review it.

How accurate it is

{% include "_proof_strip.html" %} {# The registered held-out test stays the headline (PROOF.md, via proof_facts). The real-world numbers are the registered held-out part of docs/eval-realworld.md (15 arXiv papers, 1,826 equations); tests/test_landing_facts.py checks them against that file. #}

It makes mistakes, so check the equations your students rely on. On equations it does not yet beat a general chatbot given the same PDF: the tile above shows the gap. On 15 held-out research papers typeset with LaTeX (1,826 equations) it wrote 58% exactly right, against 64% for the chatbot, a difference that set is too small to detect. Its checks compare each equation with the PDF's own text and ask you about the ones that disagree. They catch some wrong equations, not all.

What happens to your documents

Your file and everything made from it are deleted {{ retention_hours }} hours after you last work on them, so finish the review and download within that time. You can choose to keep a document for 7 days, seen only by you and the people you name. You can also let us look at the results for {{ opt_in_days }} days, to find and fix mistakes in the tool. You can delete a document at any time. We do not use your uploads to train models. Read the privacy notice.

Why STEM courses need this now

The Department of Justice's ADA Title II web rule asks public universities to make their online course materials accessible to students with disabilities. In STEM the hard part is the maths: a PDF equation is usually a picture or a jumble of characters to a screen reader. What the rule asks of STEM course materials.

For accessibility and disability services offices

ReadableSTEM assists the instructor. It does not replace their judgement or your office's review, and it does not say that a document meets WCAG, the ADA or Section 508. The page for accessibility offices covers where a document goes, who receives it, how long it is kept, and a pilot checklist you can print.
