Turn STEM course PDFs into accessible web pages, equations included

{# No deadline date is stated here: the rule is 28 CFR part 35, subpart H, and a date should only be added once it is checked against it and the university's own page (UX review BW9). #}

ReadableSTEM assists instructors who need to turn a STEM course PDF into an accessible web page. It reads the PDF, rebuilds equations as MathML a screen reader can speak, restores headings, writes short alt text for figures, and returns one web page. Every change it is unsure about goes to a review screen where the instructor accepts, edits or rejects it.

The Department of Justice's ADA Title II web rule asks public universities to make their online course materials accessible to students with disabilities. In STEM the hard part is the maths. Upload a PDF of your own notes, slides or problem sets, and get back one web page where:

  • equations are real maths (MathML) that a screen reader reads aloud, for example “v equals d over t”, and lets students explore,
  • headings are real headings, in a repaired outline,
  • figures have short alt text, or a clear flag where a description is still needed.

How long it takes

Converting takes a few minutes. Then you decide only what could change what a student reads; everything else is accepted for you and listed, so you can look it over. Dense maths means more decisions: on textbook sections in our held-out test it asked about one or two per page.

How accurate it is

It makes mistakes, so look over the equations your students rely on. In a pre-registered test on 15 textbook sections it had not seen (run on 2026-10-14, version 0.4.0), it found about as many equations as a general chatbot (89%), but fewer of them came out exactly right end to end (about half); given each equation on its own, about two in three did. Headings scored 0.96 and figure descriptions passed a model judge 97% of the time. Its checks against the PDF's own text catch some wrong equations, not all. The full results, including where it loses to a general chatbot.

Later changes were measured again on the same 15 sections (2026-10-21, version 0.5.0 and later): 0.596 of equations exactly right end to end, against 0.504. This is not a blind result: those sections had been examined since the registered run, so the numbers are optimistic. How it was measured.
