Turn STEM course PDFs into accessible web pages, equations included

{# No deadline date is stated here: the rule is 28 CFR part 35, subpart H, and a date should only be added once it is checked against it and the university's own page (UX review BW9). #}

The Department of Justice's ADA Title II web rule asks public universities to make their online course materials accessible to students with disabilities. In STEM the hard part is the maths. ReadableSTEM assists with it: upload a PDF of your own notes, slides or problem sets, and get back one web page where:

  • equations are real maths (MathML) that a screen reader reads aloud, for example “v equals d over t”, and lets students explore,
  • headings are real headings, in a repaired outline,
  • figures have short alt text, or a clear flag where a description is still needed.

How long it takes

Converting takes a few minutes. Then you decide only what could change what a student reads; everything else is accepted for you and listed, so you can look it over. Dense maths means more decisions: on textbook sections in our held-out test it asked about one or two per page.

How accurate it is

It makes mistakes, so look over the equations your students rely on. In a pre-registered test on 15 textbook sections it had not seen (version 0.4.0, October 2026), it found about as many equations as a general chatbot (89%), but fewer of them came out exactly right end to end (about half); given each equation on its own, about two in three did. Headings scored 0.96 and figure descriptions passed a model judge 97% of the time. Its checks against the PDF's own text catch some wrong equations, not all.
