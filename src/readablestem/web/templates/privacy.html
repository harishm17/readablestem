Privacy

This page describes how the beta works today. If it changes in a way that matters to you, see "Changes to this notice" below.

How long a document is kept, at a glance

By default it is deleted {{ retention_hours }} hours after you last worked on it. The two boxes on the upload form change that, and you can delete it sooner. The full list is under “What is stored, and for how long”.

What you may upload

Course materials you wrote yourself, or have the right to adapt, such as lecture notes, slides and problem sets. Do not upload student records, graded work, or anything else with students' personal information in it.

What is stored, and for how long

What is stored, and the exact clocks

For each upload we store: the PDF{% if office_on %} (for a Word or PowerPoint file, the PDF made from it; the Word or PowerPoint file itself is not kept){% endif %}{% if latex_source %} (for LaTeX source, the .tex file or the zip that was uploaded){% endif %} and its file name, images cut from it (equations and figures), the conversion result (proposed LaTeX, MathML, alt text and headings), the converted HTML, and your review decisions.

  • If you tick neither box on the upload form, all of it is deleted automatically {{ retention_hours }} hours after you last worked on it: after your last review decision or download, or the upload if there is none. From that moment it can no longer be opened or downloaded; an hourly clean-up then removes it from the database, normally within the hour.
  • If you tick "Keep for 7 days", it is deleted 7 days after you last worked on it instead. Nobody else looks at it. "Keep for 7 days" on the document's page does the same later, and shows the day it will then be deleted.
  • If you also tick "Also let us look at the results ({{ opt_in_days }} days)", it is deleted {{ opt_in_days }} days after upload. During that time the people who run ReadableSTEM may look at the conversion results to find and fix mistakes in the tool. Both boxes are unticked unless you tick them; “Keep for 7 days” can be made the default for new uploads in Account.
  • You can delete a document and everything made from it at any time with "Delete now". It is removed from the database straight away.
  • A PDF that is too large to convert at once is kept, as uploaded, while you choose which pages to convert. It is deleted {{ retention_hours }} hours after you uploaded it, when you discard it, or once every page has been sent to be converted. Nothing is sent to an AI service until you choose the pages. The sample document is a recorded conversion of a published textbook section, not your file: it is deleted on the same schedule as your own documents.

We also keep one line for each deleted account: a one-way hash of its random account id and the time. It identifies nobody and is used only to repeat the deletion if a backup is restored. Cookies

One cookie keeps you signed in (for up to 7 days) and protects forms against forgery. There are no analytics, advertising or third-party tracking cookies. While you type a review decision, the text is kept in your browser tab until you save it, close the tab or sign out; it is never sent to the server before you save.

What is logged

Logs and usage notes {# Platform logs: Railway keeps its own request logs; how long depends on the plan, so the sentence does not give a number. #}

The app's logs record what happened, not what was in your document: event names, random ids, page and fix counts, timings, model costs and error categories (for example unknown_command). They never contain document text, images, LaTeX, alt text, file names or your email address. Before documents are deleted, daily counts (for example, how many equations needed review) are added to totals that contain no content. Our host, Railway, also keeps its own standard logs of requests (time, address and page) for a limited time.

{# Usage events (web/events.py, FEATURE-REVIEW "Beta 6"); the number of days is EVENT_RETENTION_DAYS. #} {% if events_on %}

We also note what you do in the app, never what is in your documents: for example that you signed in, uploaded, made a review decision or downloaded, and your answers to the two optional questions after a download. Each note is a random id, a time, a category and at most a number; to notice the same file uploaded twice, it keeps a scrambled fingerprint from which the file cannot be rebuilt ("Delete now" removes it). No cookies or scripts are used for this. These notes are deleted after {{ event_days }} days, or with your account.

How it is protected

How it is protected {# Every line below is a behaviour the tests exercise (docs/security.md, tests/test_audit_*.py); keep it that way when editing. #}
  • The site is served over HTTPS only.
  • Only you can open your documents, and the people you name. You can give a named person the right to view or to review one document; they must sign in with the exact email address you entered, and you can remove it at any time. Everyone else on the beta, and the people who run it, are told "not found" for an address that is yours.
  • The converted document is shown in a sandbox that stops it running scripts.
  • An uploaded PDF is first opened in a separate short-lived process with its own time and memory limits, and is kept only in the database, never as a file on the server's disk.
  • Sign-in, uploads and daily page counts have limits that stop automated misuse.
    • {% if office_on %} {# T3-30. What the code does: a Word or PowerPoint file is checked in a separate process (macros, linked content and oversized contents are refused), written to a private temporary folder, turned into a PDF by LibreOffice running on the server with no network access of its own, and the folder is removed at once. Nothing is sent to a new company; LibreOffice runs inside the same service. #}
  • A Word or PowerPoint file is checked in a separate process, then turned into a PDF on the server itself (nothing is sent to another company for this). While that takes place, for a few seconds, it is written to a private temporary folder that is removed straight afterwards. Files with macros or with content linked from elsewhere are refused.
    • {% endif %}

Where it is processed

The app and its database run on Railway, in its United States region. Images and nearby text of equations and figures are sent to {{ recipients|selectattr("gets_documents")|map(attribute="name")|join(" and ") }} in the United States for processing, as described above. If you are outside the United States, your documents are therefore transferred there.
