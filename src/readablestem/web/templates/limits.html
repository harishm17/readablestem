What it handles, with limits

Each thing it handles, with its limit
  • Equations. Each equation's proposal is compared with the PDF's own text (letters, digits, signs and letter styles), and a difference becomes a decision for you. A mistake that agrees with the PDF's text, or a PDF with no usable text layer, can still get through. Math inside a table cell or a figure is often missed.
  • Chemistry formulas and reactions are found and read out as formulas, but it is the weakest area: in tests on textbook sections, {{ proof_facts.numbers.chem_right }} of the equations were right as chemistry in the newest ({{ proof_facts.numbers.chem_sections }} sections) and 0.56 in an earlier one (5 sections). Check every one.
  • Slides. A Beamer deck is read frame by frame: overlay steps that only add to a frame are merged, and each frame title becomes a heading. Lists in themes that draw their bullets as pictures (Warsaw, Madrid) come out as one paragraph, and a sidebar theme's outline is read as a column of text. A deck exported from PowerPoint is read page by page, with every step kept.
  • Tables. {% if tables_on %}A table the tool cannot read as a table of data stays a picture of the original, marked “Not converted”, and you are asked about it. A table without ruled lines can be missed and shown as plain text, sometimes as an equation.{% else %}Tables are not rebuilt yet: each stays a picture of the original, marked “Not converted”, and you are asked about it.{% endif %}
  • Figures. Alt text is short and says what the figure shows.{% if long_on %} A chart or a diagram can also get a longer description, and a chart a table of its values; their quality has not yet been measured on a held-out set, so read them.{% else %} A complex diagram still needs a longer description, which you write.{% endif %} For an exam or quiz, check the exam box so descriptions leave out the answers.
    • {% if image_pages_on %}
  • Handwritten notes and scanned pages are converted only when you ask. Check “Convert scanned or handwritten pages” when you upload. Each such page is then read from its picture, which is slower (about 20 to 30 seconds a page) and uses more of the server's budget than a typed page, and you check every converted equation and table before you share the file; the original page stays in the file, behind “Show the original page”, and the page says it was read from a picture, so small errors are possible. {{ image_pages_cost }} A tool can misread handwriting and small symbols. On 24 pages of real handwriting that were not used to build the feature, 57% of the equations came out exactly right, every wrong one had a decision, and it asked for about 5.7 decisions a page. On nine real papers made into scans, about 2 in 3 equations came out exactly right. A page whose only text is a watermark, a stamp or a page number over a picture or handwriting counts as scanned or handwritten too. Without the check, a PDF made only of scans or handwriting is refused before it counts against your daily pages, and in a PDF with typed pages each scanned or handwritten page is kept as an image, marked “Not converted”, and you are asked about it. Handwriting on a typed page is kept as an image either way.
    • {% else %}
  • Handwritten notes and scanned pages are not converted. A PDF made only of them is refused before it counts against your daily pages. In a PDF with typed pages, each one is kept as an image, marked “Not converted”, and you are asked about it. A page whose only text is a watermark, a stamp or a page number over a picture or handwriting counts as scanned or handwritten too. Handwriting on a typed page is kept as an image too.
    • {% endif %}
  • Two-column pages convert less well than single-column notes: check the reading order.
  • Links. A link over a picture or a drawing, or in a figure's caption, is not kept. Link text is kept as printed, even when it says nothing on its own (“click here”): only you can reword it.
  • Color. {% if colour_on %}Colored text in paragraphs keeps its color; color in headings is not kept.{% else %}Colored text is not kept: the file uses its own colors.{% endif %} Color inside figures stays in the picture.
  • Language. You can choose the language when you upload; otherwise it is worked out from the text, which needs about 100 letters of prose. When the text is too short to say, the language the PDF states is used, and failing that English is assumed. An assumed or mixed language has to be confirmed on the job page before the file counts as ready to share. A passage in another language is marked only when it is a whole paragraph or list item.
  • Comments and attached files. A comment someone added to the PDF (a sticky note, or a note on a highlight) is included as a paragraph that starts “Note on the page:”. Some comments are written for the instructor and not for students, so check them before you share the file. Files attached inside the PDF are not converted: the job page names them.

What it does not do

What it does not do
  • Hidden text is never used. Text that a tablet or a text recognition (OCR) program guessed and hid in the PDF, and text that cannot be seen when the page is shown or printed, is left out. Running OCR does not help: OCR text is often wrong, most of all in math. Some hidden text is not detected: white text on a white page, text under a drawn shape that only partly covers it, very small text, and OCR text that is visible on the page.
  • Math in headings stays as plain characters in the text, not as MathML equations: check how it reads.
  • The checks look at structure, not meaning. Every file is checked for structure (language, title, headings, lists, image text alternatives). The full axe-core accessibility test runs on our own test documents, not on yours, and nothing here replaces testing with a screen reader.
  • No certification. The tool assists you. It does not say that a document meets WCAG, the ADA, Section 508 or any other standard or law.

How much review it needs

  • Review time. How many decisions a document needs depends on what it is. In the held-out tests below: textbook sections, about 1.8 decisions a page (15 OpenStax sections, 242 pages); research papers made with LaTeX, about 5.6 a page (15 arXiv papers, 71 pages){% if image_pages_on %}; handwritten notes, about 5.7 a page (24 pages){% endif %}. Once your document is converted, its page tells you the exact count. Where the checks found nothing wrong, you can accept all the equations, or all the figures with alt text, in one step after seeing the list.

Earlier results, kept as history (older versions of the tool)
  • The first registered test (15 sections of five OpenStax textbooks, 242 pages, 2,738 equations, version 0.4.0, October 2026; full results). Equations: it found 89% of them, and about half came out exactly right end to end (0.50). Given each equation on its own, about two in three were right (0.68). Headings: F1 0.96, with every matched heading at the right level. (F1 is a score out of 1 that counts both headings missed and headings invented.) Figure descriptions: 97% passed a model judge, a second AI model asked whether each description is right (not yet checked by people). Decisions it asked for: about 1.8 per page; it asked about only a third of the equations that were wrong.
  • Research papers typeset with LaTeX, held out (15 arXiv papers, 71 pages, 1,826 equations, registered before the run): 0.58 of equations exactly right end to end. The registered comparison with the chatbots found no difference this set can detect. It asked for about 5.6 decisions a page. How it was measured.
  • The same 15 textbook sections, measured again (2026-10-21, version 0.5.0 and later): 0.596 of equations exactly right end to end, against 0.504 in the registered run. This is not a blind result: those sections had been examined since the registered run, so the numbers are optimistic. How it was measured.
  • Development set, not held out (25 arXiv papers, 114 pages, 2,350 equations, 2026-11-03): 0.735 exactly right end to end. These papers were used to build and tune the tool, so expect less on your documents. The development report.
  • Chemistry, held out (5 OpenStax chemistry sections, 377 equations): 0.56 right as chemistry, and 0.14 exactly right on the strict score. The chemistry report.
  • Handwriting, held out (24 pages, 205 expressions): 0.57 exactly right end to end, with no wrong equation left without a decision. The handwriting report.

Real course PDFs, especially ones typeset with LaTeX or exported from Word, may do worse. The evaluation report says how this was measured and what the numbers do not show.
