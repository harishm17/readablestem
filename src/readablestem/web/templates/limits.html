This list describes the beta as it works today.

What it does not do yet

  • Handwritten notes and scanned pages are not converted. A PDF made only of them is refused before it counts against your daily pages. In a PDF with typed pages, each one is kept as an image, marked “Not converted”, and you are asked about it. Handwriting on a typed page is kept as an image too.
  • Hidden text is never used. Text that a tablet or a text recognition (OCR) program guessed and hid in the PDF, and text that cannot be seen when the page is shown or printed, is left out. Running OCR does not help: OCR text is often wrong, most of all in maths. Some hidden text is not detected: white text on a white page, text under a drawn shape that only partly covers it, very small text, and OCR text that is visible on the page.
  • Chemistry notation is not converted.
  • Maths in headings and captions stays as plain characters in the text, not as MathML equations: check how it reads.
  • Tables. {% if tables_on %}A table the tool cannot read as a table of data stays a picture of the original, marked “Not converted”, and you are asked about it. A table without ruled lines can be missed and shown as plain text, sometimes as an equation.{% else %}Tables are not rebuilt yet: each stays a picture of the original, marked “Not converted”, and you are asked about it.{% endif %}
  • Links. A link over a picture or a drawing, or in a figure's caption, is not kept. Link text is kept as printed, even when it says nothing on its own (“click here”): only you can reword it.
  • Colour. {% if colour_on %}Coloured text in paragraphs keeps its colour; colour in headings is not kept.{% else %}Coloured text is not kept: the file uses its own colours.{% endif %} Colour inside figures stays in the picture.
  • Language. The language is worked out from the text and needs about 20 words of prose. A short handout that is mostly maths is assumed to be English unless the PDF says otherwise. A passage in another language is marked only when it is a whole paragraph or list item.
  • File types. {% if office_on %}PDF files, and Word (.docx) or PowerPoint (.pptx) files, which are turned into a PDF first.{% else %}Only PDF files are taken. For a Word or PowerPoint file, save a copy as a PDF first.{% endif %}
  • Two-column pages and slides convert less well than single-column notes: check the reading order.
  • Size. Up to {{ max_pages }} pages and {{ max_upload_mb }} MB per upload. {% if in_parts %}A bigger file, up to {{ source_pages }} pages and {{ upload_limit_mb }} MB, is kept for a day so you can choose which pages to convert; each part becomes a document of its own, and the parts are not joined into one file.{% else %}Split longer documents.{% endif %}
  • Your documents are deleted {{ retention_hours }} hours after you last worked on them (7 days if you choose to keep them, {{ opt_in_days }} if you also let the developer look). Download the file before then.
  • The checks look at structure, not meaning. Every file is checked for structure (language, title, headings, lists, image text alternatives). The full axe-core accessibility test runs on our own test documents, not on yours, and nothing here replaces testing with a screen reader.

How accurate it is

In a pre-registered test on 15 sections of five OpenStax textbooks it had not seen (version 0.4.0, October 2026; full results):

  • Equations: about half came out exactly right end to end (0.50); given each equation on its own, about two in three (0.68). A general chatbot (Claude Opus) did better end to end (0.62).
  • Headings: F1 0.96, with every matched heading at the right level.
  • Figure descriptions: 97% passed a model judge (not yet checked by people).
  • Decisions it asked for: about 1.8 per page; it asked about only a third of the equations that were wrong.

Earlier, on a small development sample only: three sections of one high-school physics textbook (OpenStax Physics, chapter 2), 43 PDF pages, on 9 and 12 October 2026.

  • Equations: 23 of 30 came out exactly right (95% interval: 59% to 88%).
  • Headings: all 121 were found; 120 of them at the right level.
  • Decisions it asked for: about 6 in 10 pointed at a real problem.

Real course PDFs, especially ones typeset with LaTeX or exported from Word, may do worse. The evaluation report says how this was measured and what the numbers do not show.
